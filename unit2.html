Unit 2: Navigating the Bazaar

Master the art of bargaining and friendly market interactions.

💡 Lola's Guide to Bargaining

Bargaining at traditional bazaars is a friendly interaction, not an argument. Do it with a smile! However, prices in supermarkets and pharmacies are final.

Uzbek Pronunciation Meaning Lola's Etiquette Tip
Qancha? KHAHN-chah? How much? Use this while pointing at items.
Juda qimmat joo-DAH kheem-MAHT Very expensive Say it playfully, not angrily.
Arzonroq bering ahr-zohn-ROK beh-REENG Lower the price The classic bargaining phrase.
Men olaman men oh-lah-MAHN I will take it Said when you agree on the price.
Faqat ko‘ryapman fah-QAHT koor-yahp-MAHN I'm just looking Polite way to decline a seller.
... bormi? ... BOHR-mee? Do you have...? Put the item name before 'bormi'.
Tatib ko‘rsam... tah-TEEP koor-SAHM May I taste it? Always ask before tasting nuts/fruit.
Yarim kilo yah-REEM kee-LOH Half a kilo Common measurement at markets.
Karta o‘tadimi? KAHR-tah oo-tah-dee-MEE Accept cards? Bazaars are usually cash-only!
Naqd pul NAHQD pool Cash Keep small bills for easy change.
💬 Mini Practice

You: Bu qancha?

Seller: O'n ming so'm.

You: Juda qimmat. Arzonroq bering?

Seller: Mayli, sakkiz ming.

✨ Tasting Culture

Walking through a bazaar, sellers will often offer you free samples of dried fruits, nuts, or sweets. It's a sign of pride in their goods!
