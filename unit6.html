Unit 6: Etiquette & Hospitality

Master the unwritten rules of Uzbek culture and respectful behavior.

💡 Lola's Tip: Entering a Home

Always take off your shoes before entering an Uzbek home. Dress modestly, especially when visiting religious sites. When an elder enters the room, it is highly polite to stand up slightly to show respect.

Uzbek Pronunciation Meaning Usage & Context
Ruxsatmi? rookh-SAHT-mee May I? (Permission) Ask this before taking photos of locals.
Mehmon mekh-MOHN Guest Uzbeks consider guests a gift from God.
Qani, oling! kha-NIH oh-LEENG Help yourself! Hosts will insist you eat more and more.
Osh bo‘lsin! ohsh bool-SIN Enjoy your meal! Bon appetit! Said before and after eating.
Omin! oh-MEEN Amen Cup hands and wipe face after a meal.
Uzr oo-ZUR Sorry / Excuse me Brief apology if you bump into someone.
Katta rahmat kaht-TAH rahkh-MAHT Thank you very much Used to express deep, sincere gratitude.
To‘ydim toy-DEEM I am full The only way to stop them serving you!
Qulluq qool-LOOQ Thanks (traditional) A humble, older way of saying thanks.
Poyabzal poy-ahb-ZAHL Shoes Must be left at the door without exception.
💬 Invited Inside

Host: Keling, uyga kiring!

You: Poyabzalni yechaymi? (Shoes off?)

Host: Ha, marhamat. Qani, to'rga o'ting!

🏡 The Seat of Honor (To'r)

The area furthest from the door is called the To'r. It is the most honorable seat in the room, reserved for elders and respected guests.
